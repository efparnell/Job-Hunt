
# 08 - Resume Strategy
## Executive Operating Manual
### Evan Forrester Parnell

> *"A resume is not a biography. It is a strategic document designed to convince a specific organization that the Executive has solved its problem before."*

---

# Purpose

The purpose of this chapter is to establish a repeatable philosophy for developing, selecting, and refining executive resumes.

The objective is **not** to maximize keyword matches or produce a single universal resume.

The objective is to maximize interview probability by aligning the Executive's demonstrated experience with the organization's underlying business problem.

---

# Guiding Principles

1. Tailor positioning—not the truth.
2. Optimize for relevance, not completeness.
3. Tell one coherent story.
4. Demonstrate outcomes, not responsibilities.
5. Every bullet must answer, "Why does this matter?"

---

# The Resume Pyramid

```
Business Problem
        ↓
Executive Value Proposition
        ↓
Career Narrative
        ↓
Selected Experience
        ↓
Supporting Metrics
```

A resume built from the bottom up becomes a list of jobs.

A resume built from the top down becomes a business case.

# What Never Changes

Regardless of version, every resume should reinforce the same central message:

The Executive helps organizations become more capable.

Recurring themes include:

- Built systems
- Improved execution
- Increased visibility
- Developed leaders
- Standardized operations
- Scaled organizations
- Solved cross-functional problems

These themes are the Executive's professional identity.

---

# What Changes

The emphasis—not the facts.

Examples:

Technical services:
- Operational excellence
- Multi-site leadership
- Safety
- Technical teams

Private equity:
- EBITDA improvement
- Organizational maturity
- Scaling
- Governance
- Integration

Fractional COO:
- Speed
- Assessment
- Flexibility
- Founder partnership

---

# Metrics Matter

Whenever possible, quantify outcomes.

Examples:

- Revenue growth
- Profit improvement
- Turnaround reduction
- Turnover reduction
- Audit performance
- Team size
- Locations managed
- Projects delivered

Metrics create credibility.

---

# Resume Review Checklist

Before submitting any resume ask:

- Does the opening summary address the employer's business problem?
- Are the first three bullets the strongest evidence?
- Are measurable outcomes included?
- Is unnecessary detail removed?
- Does every section reinforce the same executive narrative?

If the answer is "no," revise before applying.

---

# Common Mistakes

Avoid:

- Generic objective statements
- Duty-based bullets
- Excessive technical jargon unrelated to the role
- Trying to appeal to every employer simultaneously
- Listing every accomplishment equally

A resume succeeds by emphasizing the right accomplishments.

---

# Relationship to Cover Letters

The resume establishes credibility.

The cover letter explains relevance.

Together they should tell a single consistent story.

Neither should contradict or compete with the other.

---

# Continuous Improvement

Treat resumes as living documents.

After every interview ask:

- Which stories resonated?
- Which questions repeated?
- Which accomplishments attracted attention?
- What seemed unclear?

Feed those lessons back into the next revision.

The resume evolves with market feedback.

---

# Closing Reflection

The Executive's resume should never be viewed as a historical record.

It is a strategic communication tool.

Its purpose is not to describe everything the Executive has done.

Its purpose is to demonstrate that the Executive is uniquely equipped to solve the employer's most important operational challenges.

---

# Key Takeaways

1. Build resumes from business problems downward.
2. Maintain a portfolio rather than one universal resume.
3. Quantify outcomes whenever possible.
4. Tailor emphasis, never truth.
5. Improve resumes continuously using interview feedback.
