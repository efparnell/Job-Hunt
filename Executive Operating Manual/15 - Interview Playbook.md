
# 15 - Interview Playbook
## Executive Operating Manual
### Evan Forrester Parnell

> *"An interview is not an examination of memory. It is an executive conversation about solving business problems."*

---

# Purpose

This chapter establishes a repeatable framework for executive interviews. The objective is not to memorize answers, but to consistently communicate executive judgment, leadership philosophy, and operational capability.

Every interview should reinforce the Executive's identity as an organizational engineer who builds management systems, develops leaders, and prepares organizations for sustainable growth.

---

# Interview Philosophy

The interview begins long before the first question.

Preparation should focus on understanding:

- The business model
- Ownership structure
- Growth stage
- Industry dynamics
- Recent acquisitions
- Executive team
- Likely operational challenges
- Why this position exists

The better the diagnosis, the stronger the conversation.

---

# The Executive Mindset

Approach every interview as if meeting a future business partner.

Do not attempt to "win" the interview.

Instead:

- Understand the organization's challenges.
- Demonstrate structured thinking.
- Build mutual confidence.
- Evaluate whether the opportunity is a strategic fit.

The interview is a two-way diligence process.

---

# Core Narrative

Every interview should consistently reinforce five themes:

1. I build organizations that scale.
2. I replace heroic effort with repeatable systems.
3. I develop leaders rather than creating dependency.
4. I improve visibility through metrics and management systems.
5. I prepare organizations for their next stage of growth.

Regardless of the question, these themes should naturally appear throughout the discussion.

---

# The Executive Story Framework

Use a consistent structure when discussing accomplishments.

Situation

↓

Business Problem

↓

Actions Taken

↓

Systems Built

↓

Results

↓

Lessons Learned

Unlike a traditional STAR response, emphasize the business problem and the operating system created rather than individual effort.

# Questions to Prepare

Every executive interview should include thoughtful examples addressing:

## Leadership

- Developing managers
- Handling difficult personnel decisions
- Building accountability
- Managing change

## Operations

- Process improvement
- KPI development
- Organizational redesign
- Scaling operations
- Multi-site leadership

## Strategy

- Prioritization
- Resource allocation
- Long-term planning
- Organizational transformation

## Financial Leadership

- Budget ownership
- Profitability improvement
- Cost management
- Investment decisions

## Executive Judgment

- Difficult decisions
- Ambiguous situations
- Mistakes
- Lessons learned

---

# Questions to Ask

The quality of the Executive's questions often distinguishes strong candidates.

Examples:

- Why was this role created?
- What business problems should this person solve during the first year?
- What differentiates your highest-performing leaders?
- What operational constraints currently limit growth?
- How will success be measured?
- Where do you see the organization three years from now?

---

# First 90 Days Conversation

Whenever appropriate, discuss how the Executive typically approaches a new role:

1. Listen
2. Learn
3. Diagnose
4. Build relationships
5. Validate assumptions
6. Design improvements
7. Execute deliberately

Avoid promising immediate solutions before understanding the organization.

---

# Executive Presence

Demonstrate:

- Calm decision making
- Intellectual curiosity
- Humility
- Confidence without arrogance
- Structured communication
- Strategic thinking

The objective is to project thoughtful leadership rather than rehearsed perfection.

---

# Common Pitfalls

Avoid:

- Speaking negatively about former employers
- Overemphasizing technical details
- Talking more than listening
- Presenting universal solutions
- Trying to answer questions not asked

Executive interviews reward judgment.

---

# Interview Debrief

Immediately after every interview document:

- Questions asked
- Strong answers
- Weak answers
- Organizational observations
- Business problems identified
- Follow-up opportunities
- Lessons learned

These notes become valuable training data for future interviews.

---

# Executive Interview Checklist

Before the interview:

- Research company
- Review leadership team
- Understand ownership
- Identify likely business problems
- Prepare relevant stories
- Review financial and strategic context

After the interview:

- Send thoughtful thank-you message
- Record observations
- Update opportunity score
- Capture lessons
- Schedule follow-up if appropriate

# Closing Reflection

The strongest executive interviews resemble strategic planning sessions rather than employment discussions.

When both parties leave believing they understand each other's challenges, expectations, and philosophy, the interview has accomplished its true purpose.

---

# Key Takeaways

1. Diagnose before answering.
2. Tell stories through business problems and systems.
3. Demonstrate executive judgment.
4. Ask questions that reveal organizational maturity.
5. Treat every interview as mutual due diligence.
