
# 12 - Private Equity Strategy
## Executive Operating Manual
### Evan Forrester Parnell

> *"Private equity does not purchase companies to preserve the status quo. It purchases them to create value. The Executive's role is to build the operating system that makes that value creation possible."*

---

# Purpose

This chapter explains why private equity-backed companies represent one of the strongest long-term career paths for the Executive. It also establishes a repeatable strategy for identifying firms, networking effectively, evaluating portfolio opportunities, and positioning the Executive as a value creation leader rather than simply an operations executive.

---

# Why Private Equity?

Private equity firms invest in businesses they believe can become substantially more valuable through disciplined execution.

While every investment thesis is unique, recurring value drivers include:

- Revenue growth
- Margin expansion
- Operational excellence
- Leadership upgrades
- Acquisition integration
- Systems and reporting
- Working capital improvement
- Scalable management infrastructure

These are the same organizational challenges that have appeared repeatedly throughout the Executive's career.

---

# The Executive's Fit

The Executive should not position himself as an industry specialist seeking a PE role.

Instead, position around repeatable value creation:

- Building management systems
- Creating operational cadence
- Designing KPI architectures
- Developing leadership teams
- Standardizing execution
- Improving organizational visibility
- Reducing founder dependence
- Preparing companies for the next stage of growth

The value proposition is transferable across many technical and industrial sectors.

---

# Target Investment Profiles

The strongest opportunities are likely to exist within portfolio companies that are:

- Founder-led businesses transitioning to professional management
- Buy-and-build platforms
- Industrial service providers
- Technical distributors
- Environmental services companies
- Equipment-intensive businesses
- Laboratory and testing businesses
- Building services and infrastructure providers

These organizations frequently require organizational engineering more than deep product expertise.

---

# Understanding the PE Lifecycle

## 1. Acquisition

The investment thesis identifies operational opportunities.

Questions:
- What must improve?
- What capabilities are missing?

## 2. Stabilization

Leadership alignment.

KPI development.

Financial visibility.

Governance.

## 3. Value Creation

Operational improvement accelerates.

Systems mature.

Margins improve.

Organic growth resumes.

Acquisitions may continue.

## 4. Exit Preparation

Institutional processes.

Leadership depth.

Predictable reporting.

Scalable operations.

Each phase benefits from disciplined operational leadership.

---

# Target Roles

Potential titles include:

- Chief Operating Officer
- General Manager
- Business Unit President
- Operating Executive
- Vice President of Operations
- Portfolio Operations Leader
- Value Creation Director
- Integration Leader

Focus on responsibilities rather than titles.

---

# Networking Strategy

Priority relationships:

1. Operating Partners
2. Portfolio CEOs
3. Executive recruiters specializing in PE
4. Value creation professionals
5. Portfolio HR leaders

Networking conversations should emphasize business problems solved rather than industries worked.

---

# Due Diligence Questions

Before pursuing a PE opportunity ask:

- What is the investment thesis?
- Where is value expected to come from?
- Is growth organic, acquisitive, or both?
- What operational challenges exist today?
- What authority will this role actually possess?
- What resources are available for transformation?

These answers determine fit more accurately than the job description.

---

# Personal Positioning Statement

"I help founder-led and growth-stage technical businesses build the management systems, leadership capability, and operational discipline required to scale successfully and create enterprise value."

This statement aligns naturally with PE value creation.

---

# Watch List Strategy

Maintain an active watch list of:

- PE firms investing in industrial and technical services
- Newly acquired portfolio companies
- Companies approaching executive transitions
- Firms with active buy-and-build strategies

Monitor acquisitions because leadership opportunities often emerge within 6–18 months after a transaction.

---

# Long-Term Career Vision

The objective is not simply to join one portfolio company.

The longer-term vision may include progression through several stages:

1. Portfolio Company Executive
2. Multi-site or Platform Leader
3. Operating Executive across multiple investments
4. Operating Partner
5. Independent board and advisory roles

Each stage builds on demonstrated value creation.

---

# Risks

Potential challenges include:

- Aggressive timelines
- High accountability
- Frequent organizational change
- Multiple stakeholders
- Performance-driven culture

These environments reward executives who embrace ambiguity while maintaining operational discipline.

---

# Closing Reflection

Private equity should not be viewed merely as another employment market.

It represents an ecosystem built around improving businesses.

Because the Executive's career has consistently centered on building stronger organizations, private equity is not a departure from prior experience—it is a natural extension of it.

---

# Key Takeaways

1. Position around value creation, not industry.
2. Understand the investment thesis before pursuing a role.
3. Build relationships with operating partners and portfolio leaders.
4. Target buy-and-build and founder-transition platforms.
5. View PE as a long-term career ecosystem rather than a single opportunity.
