
# 10 - Executive Search Strategy
## Executive Operating Manual
### Evan Forrester Parnell

> *"The objective is not to find more opportunities. The objective is to identify the few opportunities where the Executive's capabilities create exceptional value."*

---

# Purpose

This chapter documents the decision framework developed during the Executive's search. It replaces a volume-based application strategy with a quality-focused methodology grounded in market feedback.

The search evolved from asking:

> "Is this an operations job?"

to asking:

> "What business problem is this organization trying to solve, and am I the right executive to solve it?"

That shift dramatically improved the quality of opportunities pursued.

---

# Primary Objective

Optimize for:

- Interview probability
- Strategic fit
- Long-term career trajectory
- Organizational alignment

Do **not** optimize for:

- Number of applications
- Job title alone
- Salary alone
- Prestige alone

---

# The Search Funnel

```
Job Appears
      ↓
Understand the Business
      ↓
Identify the Business Problem
      ↓
Compare Against Executive Strengths
      ↓
Evaluate Industry Dependency
      ↓
Apply or Pass
      ↓
Capture Lessons Learned
```

Every decision feeds the next one.

---

# The Business Problem Test

Before reading qualifications, answer:

- Why does this role exist?
- What changed in the business?
- What outcome is leadership trying to achieve?
- What will success look like in two years?

Only then compare the Executive's background.

---

# The Target Profile

Ideal organizations typically share these characteristics:

- Founder-led or founder-influenced
- Private equity-backed or preparing for institutional growth
- $20M–300M revenue (approximate)
- Technical, industrial, environmental, or field-service businesses
- Multi-site operations
- Operational complexity increasing faster than management maturity

These companies benefit most from organizational engineering.

---

# Preferred Roles

Primary:

- COO
- General Manager
- Business Unit Leader
- VP Operations
- Director of Operations
- Regional Operations Director

Secondary:

- Fractional COO
- Operating Executive
- Integration Leader

Titles matter less than scope and business problem.

---

# Red Flags

Proceed cautiously when a posting emphasizes:

- Decades of product expertise
- Highly specialized industry credentials
- Narrow functional ownership
- PMO-only leadership
- Heavy government clearance requirements
- Deep logistics specialization
- Fundraising operations

These usually indicate a search for a specialist rather than an operator.

---

# Green Flags

Strong opportunities often include language such as:

- Build operating systems
- Professionalize operations
- Scale the organization
- Multi-site leadership
- KPI development
- ERP implementation
- Leadership development
- Operational excellence
- Founder transition
- Acquisition integration
- Continuous improvement

These align directly with the Executive's demonstrated strengths.

---

# Application Decision Matrix

| Assessment | Action |
| --- | --- |
| Strong strategic fit | Apply immediately |
| Good fit with minor gaps | Apply with tailored resume and cover letter |
| Significant industry dependence | Evaluate carefully |
| Specialist role | Pass quickly |
| Compensation below target without strategic value | Pass |

Protecting time is part of the strategy.

---

# Resume Selection

Select the resume based on the business problem:

- Technical Services
- Industrial Services
- Private Equity
- Fractional COO

Do not reuse the same resume indiscriminately.

---

# Cover Letter Philosophy

Every cover letter should answer:

"Why this Executive for this organization at this moment?"

Avoid repeating resume content.

Demonstrate understanding of the company's stage of growth.

---

# Networking Strategy

Networking is most valuable when directed toward:

- Hiring managers
- CEOs
- Operating Partners
- Portfolio executives
- Executive recruiters specializing in industrial and technical services

Networking is not a substitute for fit.

It is a multiplier when strong fit already exists.

---

# Search Cadence

Weekly rhythm:

- Review target companies
- Monitor career pages
- Review private equity acquisitions
- Evaluate new postings
- Tailor documents
- Conduct networking follow-ups
- Capture lessons

Continuous learning is part of the process.

---

# Measuring Success

Track:

- Interview rate
- Strong-fit applications
- Recruiter engagement
- Executive conversations
- Lessons learned
- Quality of opportunities

Avoid measuring success solely by application count.

---

# Strategic Pivot

One of the defining moments of the search came after repeated feedback indicated that several organizations ultimately selected candidates with deep product or industry expertise.

Rather than broadening the search, the strategy narrowed.

The focus shifted toward organizations hiring executives to improve organizational capability rather than organizations hiring specialists to leverage existing domain knowledge.

This became the central strategic insight of the search.

---

# Closing Reflection

Executive search is itself an operational system.

Like any operational system, it improves through observation, feedback, iteration, and disciplined decision-making.

The objective is not merely to obtain the next position.

It is to identify the environment in which the Executive's unique capabilities create exceptional value for the organization and long-term fulfillment for the Executive.

---

# Key Takeaways

1. Optimize for interview probability, not application volume.
2. Evaluate business problems before qualifications.
3. Protect time by passing weak-fit opportunities quickly.
4. Target organizations entering a new stage of operational maturity.
5. Let market feedback continuously refine the search strategy.
