
# 14 - Networking Strategy
## Executive Operating Manual
### Evan Forrester Parnell

> *"Networking is not about collecting contacts. It is about building trusted professional relationships before either party needs something."*

---

# Purpose

This chapter establishes a deliberate, long-term networking strategy that complements the Executive's search methodology. Networking should increase the probability of discovering high-quality opportunities, not replace disciplined evaluation.

The objective is to build a reputation, not simply a contact list.

---

# Networking Philosophy

Networking should never feel transactional.

Instead of asking:

> "Who can get me a job?"

Ask:

> "Who is solving the kinds of organizational problems I enjoy solving, and how can I build an authentic professional relationship?"

Strong relationships compound over decades.

---

# Primary Objectives

Networking should accomplish four goals:

1. Increase visibility.
2. Learn about organizations before openings become public.
3. Build long-term executive relationships.
4. Become known as a trusted operator and organizational engineer.

---

# Priority Relationship Map

## Tier 1 — Highest Priority

- Private Equity Operating Partners
- Portfolio Company CEOs
- Founder-CEOs
- Executive Recruiters specializing in industrial and technical services
- Current and former General Managers
- Business Unit Presidents

---

## Tier 2

- Functional executives
- Board members
- Industry association leaders
- Consulting partners
- Regional business leaders

---

## Tier 3

- Former colleagues
- Vendors
- Customers
- University and military alumni
- Professional acquaintances

---

# Relationship Development Framework

Relationships generally evolve through five stages:

```
Awareness
    ↓
Introduction
    ↓
Conversation
    ↓
Professional Trust
    ↓
Long-Term Relationship
```

Do not attempt to skip stages.

Trust develops through consistency.

---

# LinkedIn Strategy

LinkedIn should be treated as an executive networking platform rather than an online resume.

Recommended activities:

- Share thoughtful observations on operations and leadership.
- Congratulate contacts on promotions.
- Engage with portfolio company announcements.
- Follow target CEOs and Operating Partners.
- Publish occasional long-form operational insights.

---

# Connection Requests

Every connection request should be personalized.

Structure:

- Mention the shared context.
- Explain genuine interest.
- Keep the message brief.
- Never ask for a job in the initial request.

Objective:

Begin a conversation.

---

# Informational Conversations

Purpose:

Learn.

Not sell.

Suggested questions:

- What operational challenges are you seeing?
- What qualities distinguish successful executives in your organization?
- Where do you see growth in your industry?
- What skills are becoming more valuable?

Listen more than you speak.

---

# Executive Recruiters

Treat recruiters as long-term partners.

Maintain periodic contact.

Provide updates after:

- major accomplishments
- promotions
- certifications
- significant projects
- changes in target roles

Become easy to remember.

---

# Private Equity Networking

Priority contacts include:

- Operating Partners
- Portfolio CEOs
- Platform executives
- Executive search firms serving PE
- Portfolio HR leaders

Focus discussions around value creation and organizational capability.

---

# Networking Cadence

Weekly:

- Respond to messages.
- Review LinkedIn.
- Reach out to one new executive.

Monthly:

- Reconnect with dormant relationships.
- Review recruiter contacts.
- Update target company notes.

Quarterly:

- Review entire network.
- Remove inactive targets.
- Identify strategic gaps.

---

# Networking CRM

Maintain:

- Name
- Organization
- Role
- Date connected
- Last conversation
- Shared interests
- Next follow-up
- Opportunity potential
- Notes

Treat networking like sales pipeline management.

---

# Common Mistakes

Avoid:

- Asking for favors too early.
- Generic connection requests.
- Contacting people only when unemployed.
- Failing to follow up.
- Treating networking as a numbers game.

---

# Long-Term Reputation

The objective is to become known for:

- Integrity
- Operational excellence
- Calm leadership
- Systems thinking
- Professional generosity
- Reliable execution

Reputation compounds.

Protect it carefully.

# Closing Reflection

Networking is most effective when viewed as stewardship of professional relationships.

The strongest opportunities often emerge from trust built years before a position becomes available.

---

# Key Takeaways

1. Build relationships before opportunities exist.
2. Prioritize quality over quantity.
3. Maintain a networking cadence.
4. Use a CRM to manage executive relationships.
5. Invest continuously in professional reputation.
