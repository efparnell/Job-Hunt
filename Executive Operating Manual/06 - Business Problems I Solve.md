
# 06 - Business Problems I Solve
## Executive Operating Manual
### Evan Forrester Parnell

> *"Organizations do not hire executives because they need more leadership. They hire executives because they have business problems that current leadership can no longer solve."*

---

# Purpose

Most executives describe themselves by title, industry, or years of experience.

Exceptional executives describe themselves by the problems they solve.

This chapter defines those problems.

The objective is to provide a framework for evaluating opportunities based upon organizational need rather than job title.

---

# Executive Positioning

The Executive should never begin conversations by saying:

"I have experience in laboratories."

"I've worked in startups."

"I'm an operations executive."

Those statements describe history.

Instead, begin with business problems.

The Executive helps organizations:

- Professionalize operations.
- Scale beyond founder dependence.
- Increase organizational capacity.
- Improve execution.
- Build management systems.
- Develop leaders.
- Increase visibility into business performance.
- Prepare organizations for sustained growth.

These outcomes remain consistent regardless of industry.

---

# Problem 1 — Founder Dependency

## Symptoms

- Founder approves every important decision.
- Managers escalate routine issues.
- Customers insist on speaking directly with ownership.
- Knowledge exists primarily in one person's head.
- Growth slows because decision-making cannot scale.

## Root Cause

The organization has outgrown the operating model that created its early success.

## Executive Contribution

The Executive introduces:

- Decision rights.
- Leadership development.
- Documented operating procedures.
- Management cadence.
- Organizational accountability.

Result:

The business becomes capable of growing without increasing dependence upon the founder.

---

# Problem 2 — Operational Entropy

Every growing organization naturally accumulates complexity.

Processes diverge.

Departments optimize locally.

Meetings multiply.

Communication fragments.

Metrics become inconsistent.

The organization slowly loses coherence.

Entropy is not evidence of poor leadership.

It is evidence of growth without corresponding investment in management systems.

The Executive's role is to restore coherence.

---

# Problem 3 — Lack of Visibility

Executives cannot manage what they cannot see.

Typical symptoms:

- Conflicting reports.
- KPI disagreements.
- Spreadsheet proliferation.
- Decisions driven by anecdotes.
- Monthly surprises.

Solutions include:

- Executive dashboards.
- Standard KPIs.
- Performance cadence.
- Reporting standards.
- Root-cause reviews.

Visibility precedes accountability.

---

# Problem 4 — Organizational Debt

Operational debt accumulates quietly.

Examples:

- undocumented processes
- inconsistent training
- duplicated work
- outdated SOPs
- unclear ownership
- disconnected software
- manual reporting

Organizations often compensate through extraordinary effort.

Eventually that effort becomes unsustainable.

The Executive treats operational debt with the same urgency that finance treats financial debt.

---

# Problem 5 — Leadership Bottlenecks

Growth frequently stalls because managers remain excellent individual contributors rather than organizational leaders.

The Executive develops management capability through:

- coaching
- role clarity
- accountability
- structured one-on-ones
- operating cadence
- measurable expectations

Leadership becomes a repeatable organizational capability rather than an individual personality trait.

---

# Problem 6 — Scaling Beyond Informality

Informal communication works exceptionally well for small organizations.

It fails as organizations grow.

Scaling requires:

- documentation
- defined authority
- standardized processes
- governance
- planning cadence
- cross-functional coordination

The Executive builds these systems before crisis demands them.

---

# Problem 7 — Cross-Functional Misalignment

Many organizations optimize departments rather than businesses.

Sales succeeds.

Operations struggles.

Finance measures something different.

Marketing defines success differently again.

The Executive aligns functions around enterprise outcomes rather than departmental objectives.

---

# Problem 8 — Growth Without Infrastructure

Growth creates stress.

Stress exposes weak systems.

Organizations often respond by hiring more people.

The Executive instead asks:

"What system should exist so that we don't need another person?"

People remain essential.

However, sustainable growth depends upon multiplying organizational capability—not simply headcount.

---

# Problem 9 — Continuous Improvement Stalls

Organizations stop improving when:

- mistakes become political
- metrics become defensive
- learning stops
- experimentation disappears

The Executive builds environments where:

- problems are surfaced early
- failures become learning opportunities
- data drives discussion
- accountability and psychological safety coexist

Improvement becomes cultural rather than episodic.

---

# Problem 10 — Preparing for the Next Stage

Perhaps the most valuable contribution an executive can make is preparing an organization for challenges it has not yet encountered.

Examples include:

- acquisition integration
- geographic expansion
- executive succession
- ERP implementation
- rapid hiring
- new service lines
- institutional investment

Great executives solve today's problems while quietly preparing for tomorrow's.

---

# Decision Framework

Before pursuing any executive opportunity, ask:

1. What business problem is this organization trying to solve?

2. Does my career demonstrate repeated success solving that problem?

3. Is industry expertise truly required, or is operational capability the differentiator?

If the organization primarily seeks:

- product expertise
- narrow technical specialization
- decades in one niche

the opportunity should be evaluated cautiously.

If instead the organization needs:

- systems
- leadership
- scaling
- operational discipline
- organizational maturity

the opportunity is likely an excellent fit.

---

# The Executive's Product

Every business sells something.

The Executive's product is organizational capability.

The outputs include:

- Better leaders.
- Better systems.
- Better decisions.
- Better visibility.
- Better execution.
- Better culture.
- Better scalability.

Those outcomes increase enterprise value regardless of industry.

---

# Closing Reflection

Industries change.

Products change.

Markets change.

The underlying business problems described in this chapter appear with remarkable consistency across founder-led businesses, private equity portfolio companies, laboratories, industrial service firms, technical organizations, and growth-stage companies.

That consistency explains why the Executive's experience transfers so effectively across industries.

The profession is not tied to a sector.

It is tied to solving organizational problems.

---

# Key Takeaways

1. Position around business problems rather than industries.
2. Founder dependency is a scaling problem.
3. Operational entropy is inevitable and manageable.
4. Visibility precedes accountability.
5. Organizational capability—not technical specialization—is the Executive's product.
6. Great executives prepare organizations for the next stage before it arrives.
