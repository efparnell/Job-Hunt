
# 17 - Future Resume Roadmap
## Executive Operating Manual
### Evan Forrester Parnell

> "A resume should evolve as deliberately as the executive it represents."

---

# Purpose

This chapter defines a long-term roadmap for continuously improving the Executive's resume portfolio over the course of an entire career.

---

# Guiding Philosophy

Treat resumes as strategic assets, not static documents.

Every interview, project, promotion, consulting engagement, board appointment, acquisition, and major achievement should strengthen the portfolio.

---

# Resume Evolution Stages

## Stage 1 – Foundation (Current)

Primary executive resumes for:

- Technical / Industrial Operations
- Private Equity
- Fractional COO

Goal:
Demonstrate organizational engineering capability.

---

## Stage 2 – Market Validation

Incorporate:

- Interview feedback
- Recruiter comments
- Hiring-manager observations
- Repeated market signals

Remove weak messaging.

Double down on themes that consistently resonate.

---

## Stage 3 – Thought Leadership

Begin emphasizing:

- Published articles
- Speaking engagements
- Industry panels
- Consulting methodology
- Organizational Intelligence Engineering

The Executive becomes known for ideas as much as accomplishments.

---

## Stage 4 – Enterprise Leadership

As career progresses, shift emphasis toward:

- Enterprise transformation
- M&A integration
- Portfolio leadership
- Board interaction
- Value creation
- Executive succession

Less operational detail.

More enterprise impact.

---

## Stage 5 – Operating Partner / Board

Future resume versions should emphasize:

- Multiple transformations
- Cross-company leadership
- Capital allocation influence
- Governance
- Portfolio value creation
- Mentorship

---

# Resume Portfolio Vision

Maintain multiple living resumes:

- COO
- General Manager
- PE Portfolio Executive
- Operating Partner
- Consultant
- Board Candidate
- Speaker / Author

Each version shares one consistent narrative while emphasizing different evidence.

---

# Knowledge Capture

After every major accomplishment capture:

- Situation
- Business problem
- Actions
- Systems built
- Metrics
- Lessons learned

Immediately update the story library.

---

# Annual Review

Once each year:

- Retire outdated accomplishments.
- Refresh executive summary.
- Re-rank strongest stories.
- Update metrics.
- Archive prior versions.

---

# Editorial Wishlist

Future versions should include:

- Resume version history
- A/B testing results
- ATS guidance
- Executive branding evolution
- Story-to-resume mapping
- AI-assisted tailoring workflows

---

# Closing Reflection

The best executive resumes are not written in a weekend.

They are cultivated over decades through disciplined reflection, intentional documentation, and continuous refinement.
