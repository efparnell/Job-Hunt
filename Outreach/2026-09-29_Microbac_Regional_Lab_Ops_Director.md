## Why this one is different from everything else in the pipeline

Every other application has asked Evan to argue that operating skill transfers across industries. This one doesn't. Analytical chemistry, laboratory operations, ISO 17025, multi-site standardization, accreditation discipline — that is the job description and it is also his resume. He was thinking about hexavalent chromium unprompted the morning he decided to prioritize it. That's a real signal, not a rationalization.

**The rare combination to sell:** most candidates for this seat are scientists who have never owned a P&L, or operators who can't read a chromatogram or speak to a method. He is both. Lead with that sentence, in some form, in every piece of this.

## The pitch, three beats

1. **A lab operator who owns the number.** P&L responsibility for multiple accredited labs: annual profit plan, monthly variance, cost per sample, capacity utilization, revenue per analyst and per instrument.
2. **Quality systems as an operating advantage, not overhead.** ISO 9001 and ISO 17025 experience. An accredited lab lives or dies on defensible data, and a director who treats the quality system as the operating system is rarer than it should be.
3. **Integration.** Three acquisitions in roughly 18 months means three sets of methods, LIMS habits, pricing and QA cultures to reconcile. That is the job nobody posts a req for, and it's what he's done repeatedly.

**Where the technical fluency belongs:** in one sentence, used as proof rather than decoration. The credible version is operational, not encyclopedic. Something like: *"Short-hold-time analyses like hexavalent chromium by 218.7 are where lab operations actually get decided — the scheduling, courier timing, prep queue and instrument uptime behind them determine both turnaround time and margin."* That tells Long you know how a lab's economics and its chemistry are the same conversation. Don't recite method numbers beyond that; it reads as trying too hard, and he's a biologist who has heard it.

## The compensation problem — plan for it, don't ignore it

The posted range is **$110–130k**. His floor is $120k *after tax*, which in Maryland means roughly $160k gross. **Even the top of this range is meaningfully below his floor, by something like $30k.** This is not the Carter Machinery situation, where the top of the range at least reached his floor.

So: pursue it for fit, and get the money on the table early rather than after three rounds.

Three questions worth asking once there's genuine interest:
1. **Is the range the whole story?** Bonus, profit-share and the annual-plan structure may add materially, and a regional P&L role often has one.
2. **Does the scope justify a higher band?** If integration of three acquired labs is really part of this, that's a different job than running an existing region, and it may argue for a Senior Director title and band.
3. **What does 50% travel actually mean?** Which sites, how many nights, and does a Baltimore base reduce it? Half-time travel carries a real personal cost that should be priced in.

If the answer to all three is no, this becomes a genuine decision rather than an obvious yes — worth having that clear before he's emotionally invested. His enthusiasm here is an asset in interviews and a liability in negotiation.
