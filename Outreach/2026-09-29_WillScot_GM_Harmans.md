## What they are actually buying

A business-unit owner for a **$25–60M P&L** spanning several branches and metro markets. Branch operations managers, field service managers, field sales and customer service all report to the seat. It reports to a VP, General Manager.

Read the requirement list carefully: **8+ years of progressively responsible business-unit management** is the gate. "$5M+ P&L" is only *preferred*, and rental/leasing and branch-network experience are **preferred, not required**. There is no niche-tenure wall here, which is exactly why this one is worth real effort.

**The trap:** a WillScot GM carries revenue and a sales team, not just operations. Most of Evan's materials lead with systems: KPIs, SOPs, ERP, standardization. Here that comes second. Lead commercial, support with systems.

## Positioning: three beats, in this order

1. **Commercial P&L owner.** "I ran a business unit where I grew sales 30% and cut costs 40% in four months." Growth first, cost second — a GM who only cuts is a controller.
2. **Multi-site operator at their scale.** 8 locations, 300+ employees, a $10M operating budget. Their branch network is the same decentralized problem: inconsistent execution across sites that each think they're unique.
3. **The systems that make it repeatable.** ERP implementation, KPI and management cadence, SOP standardization, leadership development, ISO 9001 discipline. This is the *how*, not the headline.

Do **not** lead with laboratory or analytical chemistry. It's a credibility footnote here, not the story.

## Interview prep, if it moves

Have answers ready for:
- **"You haven't worked in rental/leasing."** The answer is unit economics: utilization, fleet condition, repair cost per unit, and how you'd learn their metrics in 30 days. Don't dismiss the gap; show you know the shape of it.
- **"How do you carry a sales number?"** Use the 30% growth story with the mechanism: what changed in pricing, pipeline discipline, or rep accountability.
- **Their question behind the question:** why is the seat open? Ask it directly. A branch network posting a GM req usually has a specific broken thing.
